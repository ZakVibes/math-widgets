What's going on

Each circle spins a whole number of times per loop (1, 2, 3… or backwards), and each one rides on the tip of the one before. The Fourier transform works out how big each circle must be and where it starts, so that the last tip traces your drawing.

Smooth shapes need only a few circles. Sharp points and corners need many more: compare the infinity sign with the heart's pointed tip.

What's going on

The top plot is what your speaker actually does: every note added together into one wiggly line. The Fourier transform pulls that line back apart. Each spike in the spectrum is one pure tone hiding inside the sound, so a chord shows one spike per note.

Real instruments add smaller spikes at 2×, 3×, 4×… each note's pitch. These overtones are why a violin and a flute sound different playing the same note.

Two notes only 4 Hz apart are hard to separate: you hear one note that wobbles 4 times a second (beats), and the spectrum needs a long stretch of sound to show two spikes.
