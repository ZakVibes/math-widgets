Kac’s question

In 1966 the mathematician Mark Kac asked: can one hear the shape of a drum? If you knew every note a drum could play, could you work out its shape?

In 1992 Carolyn Gordon, David Webb and Scott Wolpert found these two drums: different shapes, exactly the same notes. So the answer is no.

Ring them

Tap a drum to strike it at that spot. Where you hit changes how loud each note is, so strikes can differ — but the set of notes is identical. Ring makes both drums play every note equally.

The drum’s patterns

Mode 1

Order and chaos

Take the drum away and roll a ball across the frame instead, bouncing off the rim. In a circle the ball’s path stays orderly forever. In a stadium it becomes chaotic: two balls that start almost identically soon go completely different ways.

Strangely, the drum remembers this. Some of the stadium drum’s high vibration patterns pile up along the ball’s unstable paths. Physicists call them quantum scars (Eric Heller, 1984), because the same equation describes a quantum particle trapped in this shape.

How to listen

Play the mystery, then Listen to each shape. Compare the lowest note first, then how the notes climb.

How it works: the drum skin is cut into about three thousand tiny cells, and the page finds its forty lowest vibration patterns. A strike is a mix of those patterns, weighted by how much each one moves at the spot you hit. All of it runs here in your browser.
